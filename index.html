Mathematical Foundation & Background Info

1. What is a Galton Board?

Invented by Sir Francis Galton in 1889, the Galton Board (or Plinko machine) is a physical demonstration of how random physical events assemble into predictable statistical patterns. As marbles fall through a triangular grid of pegs, each peg forces a marble to bounce either to the Left or Right.

2. Bernoulli Trials & The Binomial Distribution B(n, p)

Each peg bounce represents an independent Bernoulli Trial with two possible outcomes:

  • Right (R): Occurs with probability p (Success).
  • Left (L): Occurs with probability 1 - p (Failure).

For a board with n rows of pegs, each marble undergoes exactly n independent bounces. The final bin index k (where 0 ≤ k ≤ n) equals the total number of Right bounces.

Probability Mass Function (PMF):
P(X = k) = C(n, k) × pk × (1 - p)n - k

Where:

  • C(n, k) = n! / [k! (n - k)!] is the combination formula (Pascal's Triangle entry).
  • E[X] = n × p is the theoretical Mean (Expected Value).
  • Var(X) = n × p × (1 - p) is the theoretical Variance.

3. Connection to Pascal's Triangle

When p = 0.50, the number of distinct paths leading to bin k corresponds exactly to the k-th entry in the n-th row of Pascal's Triangle.

4. The Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

As the number of rows n increases, the discrete Binomial Distribution approaches a continuous Normal (Gaussian) Distribution curve with mean μ = np and standard deviation σ = √(np(1-p)). This explains why physical Galton boards form a smooth bell curve.
